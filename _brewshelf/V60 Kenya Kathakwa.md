## Roaster's Notes:

### Kenya Kathakwa – A Specialty Cup from the Volcanic Slopes of Mt. Kenya

The vibrant acidity, clear cup structure, and fruity depth that come to mind when one thinks of Kenya...

**Kenya Kathakwa** is a true example of **Specialty Coffee** that explains in a single cup why this perception has remained unchanged for years.

This coffee comes from the **Kathakwa Washing Station**, located on the volcanic slopes of Mt. Kenya in **Embu County**, near the border of **Kirinyaga**, one of Kenya's highly respected coffee regions. Established in 1964, the station currently serves around **1,100–1,200 smallholder producers** under the **Kibugu Farmers Cooperative Society**. Most of the farms here are smaller than 1 hectare; each producer operates with an average of 300 coffee trees, relying almost entirely on manual labor.

The station's location at an altitude of **1,550–1,700 meters** allows the coffee cherries to ripen slowly. This slow maturation process translates into a more **complex**, **layered, and bright** flavor profile in the cup. The red volcanic soils are one of the most important elements nurturing that characteristic vibrancy and clarity unique to Kenyan Coffee.

### Classic Kenyan Varietals, a Timeless Profile

Kathakwa predominantly cultivates the **SL28 and SL34** varietals. These two varietals form the foundation of the genetic heritage to which Kenyan Coffee owes its worldwide fame. They are accompanied by smaller amounts of **Batian** and **Ruiru 11**. The result is a high acidity that is well-balanced, fruity but never overwhelming, and a long, clean finish.

### Harvest and Processing

The clarity and clean structure of **Kenya Kathakwa** in the cup begins with the meticulousness of the harvesting process. During the harvest period, producers hand-pick only the **fully ripened coffee cherries**. This selective harvesting forms the foundation of the distinct aromas and vibrant structure characteristic of Kenyan Coffee.

The harvested cherries are delivered to the **Kathakwa Factory** on the same day. Here, the coffees are first **hand-sorted**, then depulped and transferred to fermentation tanks. The fermentation process, lasting **12 to 16 hours**, ensures the removal of undesirable flavors from the beans' inner structure and allows the cup to achieve a much clearer character.

Following fermentation, the coffees are washed with clean water, and the beans, now in parchment form, are left to dry on **raised African beds**. Throughout the drying process, workers regularly turn the beans to ensure **even drying**. During the hottest hours of the day and overnight, the coffees are covered so that the drying progresses in a controlled and slow manner. This careful process prevents sudden temperature changes and moisture from negatively impacting the coffee's quality.

Depending on the weather conditions, this slow drying phase lasts **7 to 14 days**, allowing the development of that **clean, bright, and balanced** character for which Kathakwa coffees are known.

This coffee expresses itself very clearly, especially in **filter coffee** brewing methods.

With methods like **V60**, **Origami**, **Kalita**, or **Orea**, the citrus nuances, red fruits, and the "juicy yet bright" structure unique to Kenya come to the forefront. It is a perfect match for those seeking a crisp, transparent, and well-defined cup.

### Why This Coffee?

For us, this bean demonstrates not the "loud" side of Kenya, but rather **how refined a well-processed and correctly roasted Kenyan coffee can be**. The goal in the roasting profile is not to sharpen the acidity, but to **balance** it, creating a cup that can offer sweetness, clarity, and a long finish all at once.
